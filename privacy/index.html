Privacy Policy

Effective: November 9, 2026 · Last updated: November 9, 2026

Spoilies is an iMessage app made by Spoilies LLC, a Colorado-based company. This policy explains what information Spoilies uses, why, and the choices you have. If anything here is unclear, email us at privacy@spoilies.app.

The short version

  • We can't read your chats. Messages sent in Spoilies are encrypted on your iPhone using Apple's built-in cryptographic algorithms with AES-256, the same encryption standard Apple uses for iMessage. Only people in your iMessage conversation have the key to decrypt them: not us, and not any of our hosting providers.
  • No account needed. We don't need your email, phone number, Apple ID, or any other identifying information. Your messages are stored with a random ID that isn't linked to who you are.
  • Anonymous stats, unless you turn them off. Spoilies adds to anonymous counts for each movie and TV episode: how many people checked in, how they rated it, and how much people talked about it. The counts never include who you are or anything you wrote. We use them for analysis: to see what people are watching and talking about, and to make Spoilies better. You can turn them off at any time in Settings.
  • No tracking across other apps, and we don't sell your personal data.

What Spoilies stores, and why

Your encrypted messages

When you check in, rate something, comment, react, open a chat or are typing, your iPhone encrypts it with AES-256 and sends it to our server so everyone in the conversation can see it. Check-ins, ratings, comments and reactions are saved on our server, so every conversation is kept up to date.

  • We can't read your messages or your name. What you post (your messages, your name, and details like which movie or show it's about and your rating) is encrypted before it's stored on our servers. Separately, unless you turn it off, we keep anonymous counts of which movies and shows are being watched, rated and talked about (see "Anonymous stats" below). Those counts never include what you wrote, who you are or which chat you're in.
  • What our server can see: that a message encrypted with AES-256 arrived, how big it is, when it arrived, a random code for the conversation, and your random ID (see "No account needed" below). Because your random ID is on everything you post, our server can tell which conversations it posts in and which other random IDs post there too, but not who any of them are.
  • "Here" and "typing": when you have a chat open, your friends can see that you're there or typing. Your iPhone sends a short note for this, also AES-256 encrypted, and deletes it when you leave the chat. If your iPhone can't delete the note (for example, because Spoilies closed suddenly or lost signal), friends stop seeing it within seconds and our server deletes it within about a day. You can turn this off in Settings with Show Chat Activity.
  • The key that unlocks a conversation is shared only through iMessage, which Apple encrypts end to end. If someone leaves the conversation, Spoilies makes a new key the next time someone still in it sends a Spoilies card. From then on, the person who left can't read anything new; they can still read what was sent before.

Like any website or app, our server also sees your internet (IP) address whenever your iPhone connects to it (for messages, including "here" and "typing", and for stats), because it needs that to send a reply. We don't keep it with your messages or your stats, and we don't use it to identify you.

No account needed

The first time you open Spoilies, it creates a random ID for Spoilies on your iPhone. It's how our server recognizes "this is the same Spoilies as before" without knowing who you are. It isn't linked to your name, phone number, email or Apple ID. It's kept in your iPhone's secure keychain, which only Spoilies can use, so it stays if you delete and reinstall Spoilies.

Anonymous stats

To see which movies and shows people are watching and talking about, Spoilies keeps anonymous counts.

  • What's counted: for each movie and episode, things like check-ins, ratings and vibes, Want to Watch, and how much people talk about it (how many messages, never what they say). Your iPhone works these out from its own copy of the chat and sends only the counts. Spoilies also transmits and stores information on how it's running, like which versions are in use and any errors, so we can fix problems.
  • What's kept: only totals, by movie or episode, hour and country (from your iPhone's region setting, never your location). Counts aren't stored with any ID that could identify you. We will never try to determine who is behind a total.
  • Our reports only show totals of 10 or more. This is called a data threshold. Aggregated data under this threshold is not explicitly shown; it appears as "<10" (fewer than 10) in our reports.
  • It's your choice. When you first set your name, "Share Anonymous Movie Stats" is switched on under "Your privacy settings," but nothing is counted until you tap Save and Confirm. You can switch it off there, or later in Settings; the switch covers TV and Spoilies' own counts too. We count how many people keep it on or switch it off (again, without knowing who). While it's off, your iPhone adds nothing else, and anything still waiting to be sent is dropped.
  • Your friends' iPhones count too. Switching it off means nothing you do is counted: not your ratings, check-ins, messages or anything else. Friends who share stats still count what they do, and some of their counts describe the conversation at that moment (like "this was the chat's 20th message today"), which can take your messages into account. But your own messages never generate a count, and the counts never say who did what.

Movie and TV information

Search results, details and posters come from The Movie Database (TMDB). TMDB sees what you search for, which movies and shows your iPhone loads details and posters for (for example, when you open one), and your internet address, but not who you are or which conversation you're in. TMDB's own privacy policy covers those requests.

Keeping out fake traffic

Spoilies uses Apple's DeviceCheck (through Firebase App Check) to help make sure requests to our server come from Spoilies on a real iPhone. It doesn't tell us who you are.

Crash reports

Spoilies doesn't have its own crash reporter. If you've chosen in your iPhone's settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may send us crash reports, which don't include your name.

Our website

Our home page, spoilies.app, can use Google Analytics to count visits: which pages are viewed, the type of device and browser, and the rough area based on your internet address. It only runs if you tap Accept in the cookie banner. If you tap Decline, no analytics cookies are set. It only runs on the home page, never on the pages that open Spoilies cards, so nothing from your chats can reach it. You can change your choice any time with "Cookie settings" at the bottom of the page. Google keeps the detailed visit data for 14 months; summary reports, like visits per month, can be kept longer. We also use Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools to see how people find the site in search; they don't add anything to the pages you visit.

What we don't collect

Spoilies never reads your contacts, your other messages, your location or your photos, and never asks for your email, phone number or Apple ID (if you email us, we'll have your address so we can reply). Spoilies can only see its own cards in a conversation, what you type into Spoilies, and the anonymous codes Messages gives each person in the conversation (these aren't phone numbers or Apple IDs).

How we use it

  • To run Spoilies: keeping your conversations in sync and showing who's there.
  • To keep Spoilies safe and working: blocking fake traffic and fixing errors.
  • For analysis: to understand what people are watching and talking about, from anonymous totals (unless you turn them off), and to make Spoilies better.

Where the data lives

On your iPhone

  • Your name in Spoilies, your chat history, the list of what you've watched, and your settings.
  • The keys to your conversations and your random ID, in your iPhone's secure keychain.
  • All of it goes into your iPhone's backups, if you back it up. That's how your chats come back on a new iPhone.

On your friends' iPhones

What you post in your conversations, like your name, ratings and messages, so your friends can see it. The Spoilies cards you send are regular iMessages, so they stay in Messages like any other message.

On our servers

Our hosting providers run our servers in the United States and work for us under a data protection agreement. The servers hold:

  • what you post, like messages, check-ins and ratings, encrypted with AES-256, so your friends' iPhones can get it;
  • "here" and "typing" notes, also AES-256 encrypted, while you have a chat open;
  • the anonymous stats totals;
  • a record of each deletion request we've handled.

With services that help run Spoilies

We share information only with these services, and only what each one needs to do its job:

  • TMDB provides movie and TV information (see "Movie and TV information").
  • Apple delivers Spoilies cards through iMessage and provides DeviceCheck.
  • Our website's hosts serve spoilies.app and see visitors' internet addresses, like any website host.
  • Google Analytics counts visits to our home page, only if you tap Accept (see "Our website").

If the law requires it

We would hand over information only when legally required, and only what our servers hold (above), never what your messages say.

How long we keep it

  • Chat messages, encrypted with AES-256: for as long as Spoilies runs, because anyone in the conversation can open its full history at any time, even on a new iPhone. You can ask us to delete yours (see below).
  • "Here" and "typing" notes: until you leave the chat, or about a day at most.
  • Anonymous totals: kept to show trends over time. Older hourly totals may be combined into daily ones.
  • Deletion requests: we keep a record that we handled your request (your random ID and the dates) for 3 years.
  • On your iPhone: until you request data deletion. Removing Spoilies clears most of it (see below).

Your choices

  • Turn off Share Anonymous Movie Stats or Show Chat Activity in Settings.
  • Delete your data: in Settings, tap Request Data Deletion. Your iPhone is cleared right away. Within 30 days we delete your messages, check-ins, ratings, vibes, likes and Want to Watch marks from our server. Your friends' iPhones remove their copies the next time they open Spoilies, and replies to your messages will show "Deleted message." We keep two things, both with your random ID: a small note, also AES-256 encrypted, in each of your conversations that tells your friends' iPhones to do this, and the record described above. Anonymous totals aren't affected, because nothing in them identifies you. Spoilies cards you already sent stay in Messages, like any other message.
  • Removing Spoilies from your iPhone removes your name, chat history and settings from that iPhone. It doesn't touch the copies on our server (encrypted with AES-256) or on your friends' iPhones, and your iPhone keeps the keys to your conversations and your random ID in its keychain, so reinstalling brings your chats back. To remove everything, request data deletion first.
  • Questions or requests: email privacy@spoilies.app. We'll reply within one month (within 45 days for requests under US state laws).

If you're in the EU or UK

The legal basis for each use (EU and UK law ask us to tell you this):

  • To provide Spoilies (your encrypted messages, including "here" and "typing", your random ID, searches, deletion requests): because it's needed to run the app you chose to use.
  • To keep Spoilies secure (blocking fake traffic): because we have a legitimate interest in a safe, reliable app.
  • Anonymous stats (including Spoilies' own version and error counts): because you agreed, by confirming your privacy settings with the setting on. You can withdraw that at any time in Settings; it doesn't affect counts already added.

Your rights: you can ask to see, correct, delete, or limit how we use your personal data, ask for a copy in a portable form, and object to how we use it. Where we rely on our legitimate interests, you can object at any time. Because Spoilies has no accounts and your messages are encrypted with AES-256, we often can't tell which records are yours. Deletion works through Request Data Deletion in Settings; for anything else, email us and we'll explain what we can and can't find. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Where your information is stored: Spoilies LLC and our servers are in the United States, so your information is handled there. US law may protect it differently from where you live. Our hosting providers handle it for us under agreements that include the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.

If you're in the US

Wherever you live in the US, you can ask to know, see, correct or delete your personal information. Residents of states with privacy laws, such as California, Colorado, Virginia and Connecticut, also have these rights by law, along with the right to opt out of the sale of personal information, its use for targeted advertising, or profiling.

  • We don't sell your personal information or use it for targeted advertising, so there's nothing to opt out of, and a Global Privacy Control signal from your browser or device changes nothing.
  • To delete your data, use Request Data Deletion in Settings. For anything else, email privacy@spoilies.app. Someone you authorize can ask for you, and we won't treat you differently for asking.
  • If we say no, reply to our answer to appeal, and we'll respond within 45 days. If you still disagree, you can contact your state's attorney general.

Keeping it secure

Your messages and your name are encrypted with AES-256 on your iPhone before they're sent (see "Your encrypted messages" for how the key is shared). App Check helps keep out fake traffic, and our server's rules stop anyone from reading or changing records they shouldn't. No system is perfectly secure, which is why most of what we store is encrypted with AES-256 before it reaches us.

Children

Spoilies is for people 13 and older. It isn't made for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them. If we learn that a child under 13 has used Spoilies, we'll delete what we can connect to them. If you think a child under 13 is using Spoilies, email privacy@spoilies.app.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update this page and the dates at the top. If it's a significant change, such as collecting something new, we'll tell you in the app before it takes effect.
